## Executive Summary

12th & Good Street enters a **$1.2B+ projected** U.S. employer financial wellness market that is large, fragmented (236 tracked vendors), and rapidly consolidating. The core thesis — that most platforms have structural conflicts of interest — is **verified and well-documented**. Industry buyer guides explicitly warn employers about it.

But "conflict-free" alone is not enough. Three established competitors already claim this positioning: **LearnLux**, **Financial Finesse**, and **Your Money Line**. 12th & Good Street must differentiate on its **marketplace model**, **local California advisor network**, and **coach quality curation** — not just the absence of product sales.

The window is narrowing. Nayya acquired Northstar for its wealth+health AI play. Origin raised $30M in March 2026. Well-capitalized platforms are buying market share.

## 1. The Conflict-of-Interest Problem Is Real (and Documented)

**Confidence: HIGH** (multiple 3-0 verification votes, corroborated across sources)

Most workplace financial wellness platforms generate revenue through product sales, commissions, referral fees, or loan interest — not coaching fees. This is not 12th & Good Street speculation; it's an industry-documented problem.

**Evidence:**
- LearnLux's own analysis states most platforms *"make money when employees buy a product, move money to a partner, or take a loan."*
- The SelectSoftwareReviews buyer guide warns employers: *"Many financial wellness platforms are owned by larger financial institutions, so the advice they give may be biased toward the parent company's financial products."*
- SoFi at Work explicitly sells student loan refinancing, personal loans, mortgages, investing, banking, and insurance through its workplace channel. SoFi's website states: *"SoFi is NOT a fiduciary."*
- FinFit offers credit access and personal loans alongside financial coaching.

**What this means for Tony's pitch:** This is your strongest opening line with both advisors and employers. Don't just say "we're conflict-free" — show them the landscape. The buyer guides do the selling for you.

Sources: [LearnLux](https://www.learnlux.com/post/are-financial-wellness-platforms-fiduciaries), [SelectSoftwareReviews](https://www.selectsoftwarereviews.com/buyer-guide/employee-financial-wellness-platforms), [Shortlister](https://www.myshortlister.com/financial-wellness-2/vendor-list)

## 2. Your Direct Competitors (The Other "Conflict-Free" Players)

**Confidence: HIGH** (all underlying claims 3-0 or 2-1)

Three companies already occupy the conflict-free positioning. You need to know them cold.

### LearnLux
- **Model:** PEPM (per-employee-per-month) pricing with CFP professionals
- **Positioning:** Fiduciary financial planning platform; coaches "never sell products or earn fees based on employee decisions"
- **Funding:** Series A funded (amount unverified — claim of $14.9M was refuted in verification)
- **Weakness:** National/remote-first model. No local advisor relationships. One-size-fits-all platform, not a curated marketplace. Limited Shortlister reviews suggest they may struggle with SMB market penetration.

### Financial Finesse
- **Model:** Independent coaching since 1999. Coaches earn no commissions.
- **Positioning:** "Independent unbiased financial coaching" with "unlimited access to certified financial coaches"
- **Track Record:** Longest-running conflict-free player. Has the credibility of 27 years in market.
- **Weakness:** Only 4.0 stars from 1 review on Shortlister — suggests limited market traction or visibility despite longevity. Legacy model, not technology-forward. Not a marketplace connecting employers to local advisors.

### Your Money Line ← *Most directly comparable*
- **Model:** $3-6 PEPM, strict no-sales policy
- **Target:** 500-2000 employees (same mid-market as 12th & Good Street)
- **Positioning:** Trust-building through product-agnostic financial guidance. Experts "do not promote or sell any investment products."
- **Clients:** Swire Coca-Cola, UT Health (may also serve larger orgs)
- **Weakness:** National model, no California-specific depth. Single-source pricing data (SelectSoftwareReviews), not confirmed on their own site. Not a marketplace — they employ their own advisors.

**12th & Good Street's differentiation against all three:**
1. **Marketplace model** — you curate and connect, not employ. Advisors keep their independence. Employers get choice.
2. **Local advisor network** — face-to-face capability in California. National platforms can't do this.
3. **Coach quality curation** — your Quad-A vetting process (fiduciary, fee-only, no AUM conflicts) is more rigorous than "we don't sell products."
4. **California expertise** — CalSavers compliance, state-specific tax and benefits knowledge, local market understanding.

## 4. Market Demand Is Strong — Here Are Your Stats

**Confidence: HIGH** (PwC 2026 survey, ~3,500 respondents, all claims 3-0)

From PwC's April 2026 Employee Financial Wellness Survey:

- **59%** of employees report financial stress
- **85%** of Gen Z say financial stress affects their mental health
- **71%** of Gen Z report reduced productivity from financial stress
- **52%** don't feel capable of planning long-term goals
- **41%** say their education didn't prepare them for financial management
- **83%** of Gen Z with employer financial wellness access actually use it
- **79%** of millennials with access actually use it

**For your employer pitch:** "83% of your youngest workers will actually use this. That's not a benefit that sits on a shelf — that's a benefit that drives retention."

**For your advisor pitch:** "The demand is there. PwC just published it. 59% of employees are stressed about money and their employers are looking for solutions that aren't trying to sell them a product."

**Caveat:** PwC sells financial wellness consulting, so they have incentive to emphasize stress figures. Still, the survey methodology is credible (Big Four, large sample).

Source: [PwC 2026 Employee Financial Wellness Survey](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/consulting/human-resources/library/employee-financial-wellness-survey.html)

- Investors: Notion Capital, Felix Capital, HSBC Innovation Banking UK

**What this means for 12th & Good Street:** The window for establishing market position is narrowing. Well-capitalized platforms are acquiring smaller players. This is both a threat (more competition, higher stakes) and validation (the market is real enough for $40M+ acquisitions). It also means potential exit/acquisition opportunities if 12th & Good Street establishes a defensible California mid-market position.

## 8. Actionable Playbook

### For Advisor Recruitment Calls (This Weekend)

**Your pitch to advisors should hit these beats:**

1. **"The market is real."** $1.2B+ projected, 59% of employees stressed about money, 83% of Gen Z actually use these programs when offered. This isn't speculative.

2. **"Most platforms have conflicts."** SoFi sells loans. FinFit offers credit. Fidelity and Prudential use "wellness" as a product funnel. The buyer guides literally warn employers about this.

3. **"We're building the marketplace for advisors like you."** Fee-only, fiduciary advisors are locked out of the employer channel because the platforms either employ their own coaches or sell products. 12th & Good Street gives independent advisors access to employer-sponsored clients without compromising their independence.

4. **"The window is closing."** Nayya just bought Northstar. Origin raised $30M. The consolidation wave is coming for this market. Early advisors on 12th & Good Street get first-mover advantage in California.

5. **"Here's the competitive map."** Show them this report. Three other conflict-free players exist (LearnLux, Financial Finesse, Your Money Line) — but none of them are marketplaces, none have local California depth, and none are building a curated advisor network.

### For Employer Pitches

**Lead with the problem, not the solution:**

1. "59% of your employees are financially stressed. 71% of your Gen Z workers say it's reducing their productivity. That's not a wellness issue — that's a P&L issue."

2. "Most financial wellness vendors are going to try to sell your employees products. The buyer guides warn about this. We don't do that."

3. "We connect your employees with local, vetted, fiduciary financial coaches who work for them, not for us."

4. "Your Money Line charges $3-6 per employee per month for phone-based national coaches. We give you face-to-face access to certified California advisors." *(Adjust when you finalize pricing.)*

### Pricing Benchmark

Your Money Line at $3-6 PEPM is your closest comp. For California mid-market with local advisor access, you could justifiably price at the upper end or above — $5-8 PEPM — given the premium of in-person, curated, local coaching vs. a national phone line.

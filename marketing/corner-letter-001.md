Hi — Tony here.

You joined the Corner, which means somewhere between eight questions and a
walk up Good Street, something felt like it was talking to you. Good.
That's the whole idea. Welcome — you live here now.

Let me tell you why this corner exists.

When I was 12, the man from the power company knocked on our door on 12th
Street. My mom was at work. He was kind about it — but a kid can't write a
check, and the bill was due. The lights went off, and I sat in the dark
waiting for her to come home.

Here's the part that took me twenty years to understand: we weren't
careless with money. We just had no one to talk to about it. No advisor,
no playbook, no one a step ahead of us. Every family like mine was making
it up alone, in the dark — sometimes literally.

This letter is one of the ways we fix that. Most weeks I'll send you one
real money conversation — the kind nobody had with my family. Plain
English. No products, no pitch, nothing to sell you. If a letter ever
isn't worth your two minutes, the door's at the bottom — one click, no
hard feelings.

**This week's conversation: name your number.**

Most money stress isn't about math — it's about fog. So here's the move:
pick ONE thing you're carrying (the family member you help, the debt, the
house you're saving for — whatever came up in your Blueprint) and put a
real number on it. Not "I help my mom when she needs it" — "$400 a month."
Not "I want to buy a house someday" — "$18,000 by next summer."

A number you've named is a thing you can plan around. A number in the fog
runs your life from the shadows. Write yours down this week — on paper,
in your notes app, anywhere. That's it. That's the whole assignment.

Next issue: what to do with a number once you've named it.

Talk soon —
Tony
12th & Good Street

*P.S. — If you named your number and thought "now what?" — that's exactly
what a session is for. Bring the number, leave with the plan. And if your
first session isn't worth every dollar, you don't pay.*
